# Result and claim ledger

## Supported within the frozen analysis

- The Spain analytic sample contains 29,588 students from 965 sampled schools.
- Weighted prevalence of the descriptive 0–4 life-satisfaction category is 14.58%.
- At 10% weighted capacity, behavioural–progression logistic recall is 16.3% and expanded logistic recall is 26.5%.
- The two logistic priority sets overlap little: 3.63% of total weight is selected by both, and their union occupies 16.37%.
- Among students in the target category, 66.9% are outside both priority sets.
- With priority sets reselected in each school bootstrap replicate, the mean life-satisfaction contrast between expanded-only and behavioural–progression-only groups is −0.283, with a 95% percentile interval from −0.524 to −0.004.

## Negative or qualified findings

- Histogram-based gradient boosting did not meet the prespecified rule for a robust practical advantage over expanded logistic regression.
- Higher aggregate recall did not imply similar calibration across sex, immigrant-background, or ESCS groups.
- Increasing hypothetical capacity did not eliminate ranking-related misses.

## Claims not made

- No causal effect of belonging, bullying exposure, teacher support, attendance, or grade repetition.
- No clinical screening validity, diagnosis, service eligibility, or actual referral performance.
- No evidence that a ranking improves support access or student outcomes.
- No generalisation from Spain to another education system without a new local audit.
- No independent external replication merely because the public code and aggregate tables pass internal checks.
