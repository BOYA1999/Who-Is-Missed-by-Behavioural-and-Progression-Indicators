# Analysis campaign summary

## Stable findings

1. **The information-tier result survives the continuous outcome.** Expanded HGB RMSE was 2.083 versus 2.244 for routine HGB; expanded ridge RMSE was 2.122 versus 2.245. Thus, the gain from school-experience scales is not created only by the 0–4 cutoff.
2. **Fold-wise imputation is not driving the binary result.** On 27,937 complete cases, retrained expanded HGB AUPRC was 0.327 and 10% capacity recall was 0.300; the original main predictions restricted to the same students gave 0.328 and 0.296.
3. **The school-level capacity conclusion is stable.** Expanded HGB recall was 0.281 under one national 10% capacity and 0.267 when every school received its own 10% capacity.

## Narrowed findings

4. **Adding audit attributes has limited operational value.** Expanded-audit HGB AUPRC was 0.349 versus 0.329 without those attributes; recall was 0.293 versus 0.281. Recall-gap changes were sex +0.182, immigration +0.043, and ESCS +0.076. These variables should remain audit variables rather than operational ranking inputs.

## Campaign conclusion

The main proxy-audit claim is strengthened: routine visible information is weak, richer school-experience information helps but still misses most cases, and this pattern survives continuous scoring, complete-case analysis and school-local capacity. The complex-model superiority claim remains narrow: nonlinear performance is somewhat better, but the practical gain over a transparent model is small. No further model-family expansion is justified.
